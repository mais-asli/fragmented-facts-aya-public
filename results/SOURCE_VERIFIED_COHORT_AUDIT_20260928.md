# Source-backed Aya cohorts: data and protocol audit

This is a record of the new data extension, separate from the 57-fact pilot, 40-fact held-out cohort, and 26-fact Google-RE external cohort. Selection rules, prompts, distractors, tokenizer hashes, and the analysis plan were saved before any outputs from these new Aya runs. The existing results remain part of the study.

The initial GPU launch hit a settings-loader bug before any source-cohort answer was returned. Its timestamped runtime correction, preserved first-launch code/protocols, and exactly hash-guarded retry are documented in `results/AYA_RUNTIME_ERRATUM_20260928.md`. No data selection, prompt, distractor, or analysis decision changed in that correction.

## Arabic city-to-country cohort

- Starting source: 3,362 gold-marked proper names in CAMeL CP-WIKI-D3K. We linked eligible Arabic names to Wikipedia/Wikidata entities, then to populated places in GeoNames cities500. The city identity required matching the Wikidata GeoNames ID, a matching English city name or alias, and agreement between GeoNames country code and the Wikidata country's ISO code. The Arabic country answer and accepted aliases come from Unicode CLDR.
- Of 266 source-linked candidate facts, 59 were excluded for country-granularity or current-border concerns, 8 for missing/disagreeing country codes, and 14 for an exact place-name collision in another country. This left **185 frozen rows and 72 distinct country answers**. Aya's tokenizer protocol excluded one non-NFC subject, so **184 are eligible for the GPU run**. That exclusion was recorded before outputs.
- The marked and unmarked Arabic forms have the same base letters; the marked form is attested in the CP-WIKI-D3K gold file. The English Wikipedia title and Wikidata Arabic label helped check identity. A fixed, different-country distractor was selected before Aya output, favoring a represented neighboring country and then a similar Arabic answer length.
- An answer-consistency check found that all 185 frozen Arabic gold strings belong to their CLDR-based accepted-answer sets after the conservative normalization used for scoring, with no accepted string assigned to two represented countries. The canonical likelihood target and exact-generation key are therefore aligned.
- In the 184 Aya-eligible rows, the frozen t1 subject span averages 3.46 unmarked and 9.15 marked tokens; the paired increase has median 6 tokens (range 2–12). Every marked subject uses more tokens.
- Across the 185 source-backed Arabic name forms, the median is four combining marks per name (range two to eight); all are single-word names with at least one mark. This is a description of corpus-attested pointing, not proof of complete phonetic vocalization.
- Source links and checks are attached to every row in `data/curated/aya-external-geonames-arabic-20260928.jsonl`. The selection counts and SHA-256 hashes are in `configs/aya_external_geonames_arabic_cohort_20260928.json`.

## Hebrew locality-to-subdistrict cohort

- Starting source: 2023 Israeli Central Bureau of Statistics locality records with official locality codes and subdistricts. For each candidate, the Hebrew Wikipedia lead had to contain a pointed name whose marks could be removed to recover the unique official locality spelling. The Wikidata official locality code had to match the CBS code.
- Of **646** attested pointed-name leads, **21** with disputed administrative classification, **28** with missing/disagreeing locality-code links, and **10** whose subject name cued its answer were removed. This left a **587-row source-backed pool**. A deterministic SHA-256 order within subdistrict and round-robin selection froze **240 rows with 14 distinct subdistrict answers** before Aya output. None of the new subject QIDs overlaps the earlier E6 pilot/held-out/Google-RE subjects.
- A fixed, different subdistrict distractor was selected before Aya output, preferably from the same district. Each row includes its Wikipedia pointed-name excerpt, CBS source, Wikidata identity, and recorded checks in `data/curated/aya-external-cbs-hebrew-20260928.jsonl`. The sampling rule, exclusions, and source hashes are in `configs/aya_external_cbs_hebrew_cohort_20260928.json`.
- In the 240 Aya-eligible rows, the frozen t1 subject span averages 3.22 unmarked and 10.24 marked tokens; the paired increase has median 7 tokens (range 3–18). Every marked subject uses more tokens.
- The 240 source-attested Hebrew forms have a median of four marks per name (range two to 13). Of 135 multiword names, five have at least one word without a mark; these remain in the frozen cohort, so the intervention is source-attested pointing rather than guaranteed fully pointed orthography.

## Aya run and planned analysis

- Both new cohorts use the pinned **CohereLabs/aya-23-8B** revision `89da1a0ed02d6130f93ae0ffdbedb63b760c0471` with NF4/FP16, greedy decoding, two native-language prompt wordings, and paired unmarked/marked subject forms. The frozen protocol is `configs/aya_source_verified_external_protocol_20260928.json`.
- A local QID audit found no duplicate subjects within either curated cohort, no subject overlap between the Arabic and Hebrew cohorts, and no overlap with the 246-row earlier E6 pilot/held-out/Google-RE source set.
- The planned primary outcome, calculated separately in each language, is the marked-minus-unmarked change in the log-likelihood margin between the gold answer and a prespecified distractor under the first prompt. The analysis weights distinct answer strata equally, clusters uncertainty by answer, and adjusts the two primary p-values by Holm's method. The plan is `configs/aya_source_verified_analysis_plan_20260928.json`.
- Secondary measures are the second wording, gold-answer likelihood, exact generation accuracy, a narrowly specified answer-prefix rematch, and an English recall control for Arabic city facts. The Arabic country and Hebrew subdistrict tasks have different domains, relations, and answer sets, so their absolute accuracies are **not** a controlled language comparison.
- The 246-row E6 Aya position-decomposition run is independent of these new data extensions and is **post-hoc exploratory**. It tests resegmentation and position sensitivity but does not isolate a pure token-count effect.
- A further fixed-site activation-patching replication was submitted after E6 began running. Its frozen protocol selects 96 source-disjoint subjects per language without using Aya outcomes; the last-subject residual site at layers 12–13 comes from the earlier pilot/held-out Aya work. The primary outcome and same-entity, unrelated-donor, and identity controls are in `configs/aya_source_verified_e5_protocol_20260928.json` and `configs/aya_source_verified_e5_analysis_plan_20260928.json`. The two jobs are recorded in `results/tau_source_verified_e5_upload_20260928.json`.

## Evidence limits

Cross-source agreement strengthens identity and fact checks but does not prove that every record is error-free. The Arabic gold markings come from an annotated corpus; the Hebrew pointing is attested in Wikipedia leads. The new names were not independently adjudicated by a speaker. Current source pages and GeoNames records can also postdate Aya's training data. Source-based checking should be described as such, without assigning a person as reviewer.
